Building Consistent Strength

August 5, 2026 · Training

There's a myth that's stuck in my head for years: "If you don't hit PRs every week, you're not progressing." I've heard this from athletes, coaches, and even myself. But here's the truth: consistency beats intensity every single time.

"Progress is compound. It's not about the single best workout; it's about showing up every day and doing the work."

The Intensity Trap

Chasing maximal intensity every session creates a false sense of progress. You might PR one week, then have three weeks of stagnation. The difference? The first week looked impressive; the second, third, and fourth were a disaster.

Consistency is about showing up, not about how hard you feel in any given session.

The Consistency Framework

1. Minimum Effective Dose

Find the minimum amount of training that produces results. If you're training 6 days a week and making progress, can you drop to 4 days? If you're training 3 days a week and plateauing, can you increase to 5 days?

The goal isn't to train more; it's to find your optimal frequency that fits your life and produces progress.

2. Consistency Score (0-100)

Every week, give yourself a consistency score based on:

  • Volume completed — % of planned volume actually done
  • Intensity maintained — % of sets performed at target intensity
  • Recovery quality — sleep, stress, nutrition

This becomes your training baseline. If you score 80/100 one week, you know you can train harder next week. If you score 50/100, you know you need to dial back and recover.

3. Progressive Overload Without PRs

You don't need to PR every week. You need to progressively overload consistently. This means:

  • More reps — same weight, slightly more volume
  • More volume — same rep scheme, slightly more sets
  • Slightly higher intensity — same weight, 1-2 more reps
  • Better technique — same intensity, improved execution
  • More consistency — more training days, no missed sessions

4. The 2-Week Rule

Don't judge your progress on single weeks. If you miss a week or two, don't let it spiral. The next week, just get back on track. Treat setbacks as data points, not failures.

Practical Application

Here's how I apply this framework to my athletes:

  1. Set a weekly consistency target — 85-95% completion
  2. Track every session — use a journal or app
  3. Weekly review — look at the pattern over 4-8 weeks, not single weeks
  4. Adjust based on data — if consistency is 85%, increase volume 5-10%; if 55%, reduce volume 15-20%

Bottom Line

Consistency is the most underrated training variable. It compounds over time. It's the difference between athletes who plateau and those who progress. Don't chase intensity. Chase consistency.
